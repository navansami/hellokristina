## Hello & scripts

### Say hello (hello)

**Grammar note:** Serbian greetings change through the day: dobro jutro in the morning, dobar dan during the day and dobro veče in the evening. Laku noć is only for bedtime or leaving late at night. Zdravo and ćao are informal — use them with friends. Ćao means both hi and bye. Notice dobro / dobar: "good" changes its ending to match the word after it. Jutro and veče are neuter (dobro), dan is masculine (dobar). You will see this pattern everywhere.

| Serbian | English |
|---|---|
| zdravo | hello |
| ćao | hi / bye |
| dobro jutro | good morning |
| dobar dan | good day |
| dobro veče | good evening |
| laku noć | good night |
| doviđenja | goodbye |
| vidimo se | see you |
| hvala | thank you |
| Dobro jutro, Kristina! | Good morning, Kristina! |
| Ćao, vidimo se! | Bye, see you! |
| Dobro veče, Ana! | Good evening, Ana! |
| Laku noć i hvala! | Good night and thank you! |
| Zdravo, Marko! | Hello, Marko! |
| Doviđenja i hvala! | Goodbye and thanks! |
| Dobro **jutro**, Ana! (wrong: dan noć) | Good morning, Ana! |
| **Dobar** dan! (wrong: Dobro Laku) | Good day! |
| Laku **noć**! (wrong: jutro dan) | Good night! |
| Dobro jutro! → **Dobro jutro! Kako si?** (wrong: Laku noć! / Doviđenja!) | Good morning! |
| Ćao, vidimo se sutra! → **Ćao, vidimo se!** (wrong: Dobro jutro! / Dobar dan, drago mi je.) | Bye, see you tomorrow! |

### Be polite (polite)

**Grammar note:** Serbian has two words for "you". Use ti with friends, family and children. Use Vi with strangers, older people, shop staff and anyone in a formal situation. Vi is also the plural "you". Many polite words come in both forms: • izvini / izvinite — sorry, excuse me • hvala ti / hvala vam — thank you Molim is a multi-tool: "please", "you're welcome" and "pardon?" when you didn't catch something.

| Serbian | English |
|---|---|
| molim | please / you are welcome |
| molim vas | please (formal) |
| hvala vam | thank you (formal) |
| hvala ti | thank you (informal) |
| izvini | sorry (informal) |
| izvinite | excuse me (formal) |
| nema problema | no problem |
| da | yes |
| ne | no |
| Izvinite, molim vas. | Excuse me, please. |
| Ne, hvala. | No, thanks. |
| Da, hvala vam. | Yes, thank you. |
| Hvala ti, Ana! | Thank you, Ana! |
| Izvini, Marko! | Sorry, Marko! |
| Izvinite, **molim** vas. (wrong: da hvala) | Excuse me, please. |
| Hvala **ti**, Jelena! (wrong: vam ne) | Thank you, Jelena! (to a friend) |
| Nema **problema**. (wrong: hvala molim) | No problem. |
| Hvala vam! → **Molim.** (wrong: Izvinite. / Laku noć.) | Thank you! |
| Izvini! → **Nema problema.** (wrong: Hvala ti. / Dobro jutro.) | Sorry! |

### How are you? (questions)

**Grammar note:** Kako si? asks a friend how they are. Kako ste? is the polite form, and also the plural. Answer with Dobro sam — sam means "I am". Throw the question back with A ti? or A Vi? Some words show who is speaking: a man says umoran sam, a woman says umorna sam. The extra ‑a for women is one of the most common patterns in Serbian.

| Serbian | English |
|---|---|
| kako si | how are you? |
| kako ste | how are you? (formal) |
| dobro sam | I am fine |
| odlično | great |
| nije loše | not bad |
| tako-tako | so-so |
| umoran sam | I am tired (man) |
| umorna sam | I am tired (woman) |
| a ti | and you? |
| Kako si, Ana? | How are you, Ana? |
| Dobro sam, hvala. A ti? | I am fine, thanks. And you? |
| Odlično, hvala! | Great, thanks! |
| Kako ste danas? | How are you today? |
| Umorna sam danas. | I am tired today. |
| Dobro **sam**, hvala. (wrong: si ste) | I am fine, thanks. |
| Kako **ste**, gospodine? (wrong: si sam) | How are you, sir? |
| Ja sam Ana i **umorna** sam. (wrong: umoran odlično) | I am Ana and I am tired. |
| Kako si? → **Dobro sam, hvala. A ti?** (wrong: Zovem se Marko. / Laku noć!) | How are you? |
| Kako ste? → **Odlično, hvala. A Vi?** (wrong: Ne, hvala. / Umoran si.) | How are you? (formal) |
| Kristina: Zdravo, Miloš! Kako si? / Miloš: Nije loše, ali umoran sam. A ti? / Kristina: Odlično! | Kristina: Hi, Miloš! How are you? Miloš: Not bad, but I am tired. And you? Kristina: Great! |

### Two alphabets (script-basics)

**Grammar note:** Serbian is written in two alphabets, Cyrillic (ћирилица) and Latin (latinica). Both are official and every Serb reads both. Each letter always has the same sound, so once you know the letters you can read any word aloud. Special Latin letters: č and ć (both close to "ch"), š (sh), ž (the s in "pleasure"), đ and dž (close to "j"), lj and nj (as in "million" and "canyon"), and j, which always sounds like "y". Cyrillic look-alikes that sound different: В = v, Н = n, Р = r, С = s, Х = h.

| Serbian | English |
|---|---|
| taksi | taxi |
| restoran | restaurant |
| muzej | museum |
| banka | bank |
| pošta | post office |
| apoteka | pharmacy |
| mama | mum |
| tata | dad |
| radio | radio |
| Ovo je banka. | This is a bank. |
| Gde je pošta? | Where is the post office? |
| Taksi, molim vas! | Taxi, please! |
| Mama i tata su ovde. | Mum and dad are here. |
| Restoran je tamo. | The restaurant is over there. |
| Ovo je **muzej**. (wrong: banka radio) | This is a museum. |
| Gde je **apoteka**? (wrong: pošta taksi) | Where is the pharmacy? |
| Izvinite, gde je muzej? → **Tamo, desno.** (wrong: Dobro sam, hvala. / Ja sam muzej.) | Excuse me, where is the museum? |
| АПОТЕКА | Apoteka — pharmacy. |

## Who I am

### Meet & greet (introductions)

**Grammar note:** Zovem se literally means "I call myself". The se stays and the verb changes: zovem se (I), zoveš se (you), zove se (he / she). Ask: Kako se zoveš? (friend) or Kako se zovete? (polite). When you meet someone, say Drago mi je — "nice to meet you". The usual answer is I meni (me too). Introduce others with Ovo je…: Ovo je Ana, moja prijateljica.

|---|---|

| ti | you |

| drago mi je | nice to meet you |
| i meni | me too |
| ovo je | this is |
| prijatelj | friend (man) |
| prijateljica | friend (woman) |

| Kako se zoveš? | What is your name? |

### I am, you are (to-be)

**Grammar note:** ja sam · ti si · on / ona / ono je mi smo · vi ste · oni su The ending already tells you who, so the pronoun is often dropped: Student sam = Ja sam student. These little forms never start a sentence: say Ja sam… or Student sam…, not Sam student. Serbian has no "a" or "the": Ja sam student = I am a student.

### Where are you from? (countries)

**Grammar note:** Iz means "from" and changes the country's ending: Srbija → iz Srbije, Engleska → iz Engleske, Amerika → iz Amerike (‑a becomes ‑e). U means "in" and uses another ending: u Srbiji, u Engleskoj, u Londonu, u Beogradu. For now, learn them as fixed phrases. The pattern will click with practice.

| Engleska | England |
| Amerika | America |

| London | London |
| grad | city |

**Grammar note:** You can almost always tell a noun's gender from its ending: • masculine — a consonant: sto, telefon, ključ • feminine — ‑a: knjiga, torba, olovka • neuter — ‑o or ‑e: pismo, ogledalo, more Gender matters because the words around a noun change to match it: moj telefon, moja knjiga, moje pismo.

Dobar loses its second a when it adds an ending: dobra, dobro. Veliki and mali usually keep their ‑i in the masculine: veliki grad, mali stan.

## Everyday verbs

### I have (have)

**Grammar note:** imam · imaš · ima · imamo · imate · imaju Imati is a regular ‑am verb. Learn it and you know hundreds of verbs that work the same way. After "have" (and most verbs), feminine nouns change ‑a to ‑u: Imam sestru, Imam mačku. Masculine things stay the same (Imam auto, Imam telefon), but people and animals add ‑a: brat → Imam brata, pas → Imam psa.

### Not and never (not)

**Grammar note:** Put ne before a verb to make it negative: ne znam, ne razumem, ne radim. Serbian loves double negatives: Nikad ne pijem kafu (I never drink coffee). The ne stays even with nikad or ništa.

| Kako je bilo na moru? → **Bilo je odlično!** (wrong: Bio je odlično! / Idemo na more.) | How was the seaside? |
| Šta ste radili u subotu? → **Išli smo na izlet.** (wrong: Idemo na izlet. / Išao smo na izlet.) | What did you do on Saturday? |
| Prošlog vikenda smo bili na Zlatiboru. U subotu smo išli na planinu, a uveče smo jeli roštilj. U nedelju je padala kiša, pa smo bili u hotelu. Bilo je lepo, ali smo bili umorni. | Last weekend we were on Zlatibor. On Saturday we went up the mountain, and in the evening we ate barbecue. On Sunday it rained, so we stayed in the hotel. It was nice, but we were tired. |

### Weather (a1-health-weather-weather-today)

**Grammar note:** Weather sentences have no "it": Toplo je (it's warm), Hladno je, Sunčano je, Oblačno je. That's the ‑o form again. Rain and snow "fall": Pada kiša, Pada sneg. Duva vetar means the wind blows. Vreme means both "weather" and "time". Seasons: proleće, leto, jesen, zima. "In summer" is leti, "in winter" is zimi.

| Serbian | English |
|---|---|
| vreme | weather / time |
| sunčano | sunny |
| pada kiša | it is raining |
| pada sneg | it is snowing |
| toplo | warm |
| hladno | cold |
| vetar | wind |
| oblačno | cloudy |
| leto | summer |
| zima | winter |
| Danas je sunčano i toplo. | Today it is sunny and warm. |
| Pada kiša, ne idem napolje. | It is raining, I am not going out. |
| Zimi je hladno i pada sneg. | In winter it is cold and it snows. |
| Kakvo je vreme u Londonu? | What is the weather like in London? |
| Duva jak vetar. | A strong wind is blowing. |
| Danas je **sunčano**. (wrong: oblačno hladno) | Today it is sunny. |
| Pada **sneg**. (wrong: kiša vetar) | It is snowing. |
| Leti je **toplo**. (wrong: hladno zima) | In summer it is warm. |
| Kakvo je vreme danas? → **Oblačno je, ali toplo.** (wrong: Oblačan sam, ali topao. / Danas je ponedeljak.) | What is the weather like today? |
| Da li pada kiša? → **Ne, sunčano je.** (wrong: Ne, sunčana je. / Kiša je moja sestra.) | Is it raining? |
| Prognoza za sutra: ujutru oblačno, posle podne pada kiša. Uveče je hladno, oko pet stepeni. | Forecast for tomorrow: cloudy in the morning, rain in the afternoon. Cold in the evening, around five degrees. |

### At the doctor (a1-health-weather-feeling-well-or-ill)

**Grammar note:** Boli me glava — literally "the head hurts me". The body part is the subject, and me (me) stays the same: • Boli me stomak • Boli me grlo • Boli me zub Ask: Šta te boli? (What hurts?) Ill: bolestan sam (man) / bolesna sam (woman). Imam temperaturu means I have a fever.

| Serbian | English |
|---|---|
| boli me | it hurts (me) |
| glava | head |
| stomak | stomach |
| grlo | throat |
| zub | tooth |
| bolestan sam | I am ill (man) |
| bolesna sam | I am ill (woman) |
| lek | medicine |
| prehlada | a cold |
| temperatura | fever / temperature |
| Boli me glava. | I have a headache. |
| Bolesna sam, imam temperaturu. | I am ill, I have a fever. |
| Treba mi lek za grlo. | I need medicine for my throat. |
| Šta te boli? | What hurts? |
| Moj sin ima prehladu. | My son has a cold. |
| Boli me **zub**. (wrong: glava lek) | My tooth hurts. |
| Marko je **bolestan**. (wrong: bolesna umorna) | Marko is ill. |
| Imam **temperaturu**. (wrong: temperatura prehlada) | I have a fever. |
| Šta te boli? → **Boli me stomak.** (wrong: Boli ja stomak. / Bolim stomak.) | What hurts? |
| Kako si danas? → **Nisam dobro, bolestan sam.** (wrong: Nisam dobro, bolestan si. / Odlično, boli me glava.) | How are you today? |
| Zara je bolesna. Boli je grlo i ima temperaturu. Ide u apoteku i kupuje lek. Danas ne ide na posao. | Zara is ill. Her throat hurts and she has a fever. She goes to the pharmacy and buys medicine. Today she is not going to work. |
